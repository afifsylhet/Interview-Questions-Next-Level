Learn these 5 interpersonal questions 

1. Tell me about yourself. 
2. Tell me about your weakness. 
3. Why should we hire you? 
4. What is your strength? 
5. Why did you choose web development as a career?
 
## 1. Tell me about yourself

"I am a full-stack developer. I have more than 3 years of experience. I build web apps using React, Next.js, Node.js, and Go.
 
Before this, I worked for 13 years in finance and data management. I worked with Johns Hopkins University and UN programs like IOM and UNICEF. That job taught me to be very careful and accurate with data.
 
Now, I work as a full-stack developer. I have built more than 20 projects — from planning to launch. Some examples: a multi-tenant ERP system, a hospital management app that works offline, and a mobile exam app that handles many students at the same time without errors.
 
Now I want to work with an international remote team, doing full-stack or backend work."
 
## 2. What is your weakness?

"I did not study computer science in university. I came from a finance background. So sometimes, deep technical topics — like advanced algorithms — take me more time to learn than someone with a CS degree.
 
To fix this, I use tools like Cursor and Copilot. But I don't just copy the code — I study it, to understand why it works. I also read the code of libraries I use. This way, I am learning and improving every day."
 
## 3. What is your strength?

"I have two main strengths.
 
First, I can handle a full project alone — from database design to the final app. For example, I built a multi-tenant ERP system from zero. It keeps each client's data safe and separate, using one codebase.
 
Second, I am very careful about data accuracy and system reliability. This comes from my 13 years in finance. Because of this habit, my hospital app never lost any patient data. And my exam app never gave duplicate scores, even when many students submitted at the same time."
 
## 4. Why should we hire you?
 
"Three reasons.
 
One — I finish what I start. I have shipped more than 20 real projects, not just practice projects.
 
Two — I already know how to work with international teams. I have worked with clients from different countries. My English is good, and I communicate well in remote/async work.
 
Three — my finance background helps me think about security and mistakes before they happen. Many developers only learn this after something breaks. I already learned it in my old job."
 
## 5. Why did you choose web development as a career?
 
"I worked in finance and data management for 13 years. During that time, I used many systems that were slow or not well-built. But I could not fix them — someone else built them.
 
I wanted to be the person who builds the solution, not just the person who uses it. Web development gave me that power. I can see a problem, and then build the fix myself.
 
I also love that in web development, you build something today, and real people can use it in a few days. That fast result is what keeps me excited."
